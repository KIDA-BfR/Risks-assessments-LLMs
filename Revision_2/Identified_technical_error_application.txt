First, in 4 of the 35 OPM runs the moderating agent's final public answer contained two deals, a primary proposal together with a fallback. However, only one of these was retained in the original extraction. Re-extracting all distinct deals from every final proposal leaves the set of most recurrent deals unchanged, but alters single occurrence count (from two runs to three), so the distribution of candidate deals presented to the workshop participants, and hence the human-in-the-loop compromise that followed, was unaffected. 

Second, the agent prompts stated that a proposal passes if at least two parties agree, a threshold inherited from the three-stakeholder scenarios used during proof-of-concept development (see Methods section). For the five-stakeholder OPM scenario the corresponding majority would have been three. This may have made agents more willing to advance proposals acceptable to a subset of parties and could have influenced their concession strategies. Its practical effect is limited, however, because the simulation output is a candidate proposal rather than a binding vote: no deal was adopted based on the stated rule and the final outcome in both case scenarios was determined by the participants during the human-in-the-loop review
